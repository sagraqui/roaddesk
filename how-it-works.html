About the project

One tour book.
Zero repeat questions.

A touring production moves dozens of people through a new city every day. The plan lives in a tour book: a long document with schedules, travel, contacts and policies. It is accurate, and it is almost never the first place a tired crew member looks. They text the tour manager instead. RoadDesk is an AI assistant that reads the tour book so the crew does not have to, and gives the production office its phone back.

The problem

Tour managers and production coordinators answer the same questions all day: call times, bus lists, hotel addresses, per diem, guest list rules. Every answer is already written down. The cost is interruption: each text pulls a decision maker away from the problems that actually need a human, and a wrong answer passed along in a group chat can mean a missed bus call.

The solution

RoadDesk is a chatbot trained only on a tour's official documents. For this pilot it serves Lumen Coast's Northern Lights Tour, Texas leg, November 19 to 29, 2026. Crew open one link, ask in plain language, and get the answer the tour book gives. When the book does not cover a question, RoadDesk says so and points to the right contact.

How it works

Four steps from PDF to crew assistant.

Upload the source documents

The production team adds the tour book, day sheets and crew policies. These are the only material RoadDesk is allowed to answer from.

Set the guardrails

RoadDesk is instructed to stay on tour topics, to quote the policy behind an answer, and to hand off to a named contact for anything about safety, pay disputes or emergencies.

Test with real questions

The tour manager runs the forty most common crew questions through it and corrects the source documents wherever an answer is unclear. Fixing the book fixes the bot.

Share one link

The assistant goes out to the crew as a link or a widget on the tour site, like the one in the corner of this page. No install, no login, works on any phone backstage.

Who it is for

Tour managers, production coordinators and crew chiefs on arena, theater and festival runs, plus the touring and local crew they support.

💰

Business model

A per-tour subscription priced by leg length and crew size. Setup takes a day, so it pays for itself on short runs as well as long ones.

🔭

What is next

Daily day-sheet updates, Spanish and English answers for cross-border crews, and usage reports that show which parts of the tour book confuse people.
